---
name: Research brief
description: Writing a short, sourced account of something you had to look up.
when: The task asks what something is, what it costs, how it works, or what happened — and the answer is not in the material provided.
---

Write for someone who will act on this and has no time to read twice.

**Lead with the answer.** The first sentence is the finding, not the setup. If
the question was "what does it cost", the first sentence contains the number.

**Attribute everything that could be wrong.** Any figure, date, claim about what
a company says or does, or statement about the future gets a citation `[n]`.
Sentences that need no citation are the ones a reader could verify themselves.

**Separate what a source states from what it implies.** "TypeSafe says output
tokens are free" is a fact about a claim. "Output tokens are free" is a claim
about the world. When only one vendor-controlled source exists, say so.

**Say what you could not establish.** A brief that quietly omits the thing the
reader most wanted is worse than one that says "no independent benchmark exists
yet". Name the gap in one sentence.

Avoid: restating the question, "in today's fast-moving landscape", bulleted
lists of adjectives, and any sentence that would survive the subject being
swapped for a different one.
